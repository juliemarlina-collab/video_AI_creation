① 4 Main Phases

Click each coloured tab to explore the full guide for that phase — what to do, what output to produce and what comes next. Always complete each phase in order before moving to the next.

Phase 01 — Master Prompting

This is where everything begins. Do not generate any image or video until this phase is complete. You write one Master Prompt that captures the full story, characters, visual style and scene plan. This prompt guides all image and video generation in Phases 02, 03 and 04.

  • Step 1: Decide your video title, topic and learning objective
  • Step 2: Identify your target audience and moral value of the story
  • Step 3: Create character profiles — name, appearance, role and personality
  • Step 4: Choose visual style, mood and colour palette (e.g. cinematic, warm, folk)
  • Step 5: Plan 20 scenes with title and 9-second time slot each (20 × 9s = 3 min)
  • Step 6: Write and save your Master Prompt — use the template in Section ② below
  • Step 7: Paste the prompt into Gemini or ChatGPT to generate a full scene-by-scene production guide
✅ Output: Master Prompt + 20-Scene Storyboard Plan

➡ Scroll to Section ② to copy the Master Prompt template, then paste it into Gemini or ChatGPT.

Teaching Direction

Topic, objective, moral value and target audience. Decide this before anything else — it shapes the whole video.

Story Design

Beginning → conflict → turning point → resolution → moral lesson. Every scene must serve the story arc.

Character Profiles

Bawang Putih, Bawang Merah, Ibu Tiri, Nenek Tua, Jejaka Moden — define each character's look once and keep it consistent in every scene.

Scene Plan

20 scenes × 9 seconds = 3 minutes exactly. Write scene title + summary for every scene before generating anything.

Phase 02 — Image Creation

Using the scene prompts from your Phase 01 storyboard, generate one still image per scene using a free AI image tool. These 20 images are the visual foundation of your video — you cannot animate what you have not first generated as a still image.

  • Step 1: Open Gemini (gemini.google.com) or Bing Image Creator (free)
  • Step 2: Use each scene's image prompt from your Phase 01 output
  • Step 3: Generate images in 16:9 landscape format for video compatibility
  • Step 4: Keep character face, outfit and setting consistent across all scenes
  • Step 5: If an image looks wrong, adjust the prompt and regenerate — do not skip
  • Step 6: Save all 20 images in one folder before moving to Phase 03
✅ Output: 20 Scene Images (16:9, one per scene)

➡ Once all 20 images are saved, check them in Section ③ Storyboard, then proceed to Section ④ to choose your Phase 03 video tool.

Free Image Tools

Gemini (gemini.google.com) · Bing Image Creator (free) · Adobe Firefly (free tier). Use the same tool for all 20 scenes.

Consistency Rule

Check outfit, background, expression and visual style are consistent. Compare Scene 1 and Scene 20 side by side before continuing.

Scene Examples

Kampung sunrise · River washing · Magical box glow · City café interior · Cherry blossom park.

Beginner Tip

Generate scenes in order (1 → 20). This makes it easier to keep continuity. Never jump to scene 10 before finishing scenes 1–9.

Phase 03 — Video Motion

Upload your 20 scene images into a free video tool and bring them to life with motion, camera moves and transitions. This phase produces a silent draft video. Do not add audio yet — that is Phase 04.

  • Step 1: Open Google Vids or Canva (both free — see Section ④)
  • Step 2: Upload and arrange all 20 scene images in the correct sequence
  • Step 3: Set each clip to 9 seconds duration
  • Step 4: Add camera movement — push-in, zoom, slow pan or static hold
  • Step 5: Add transitions between scenes — fade, dissolve or cut
  • Step 6: Watch the full silent draft (3 minutes) and check the story flows clearly
  • Step 7: Export as MP4 draft — this goes into Phase 04 for audio
✅ Output: Silent Draft Video (3 minutes, all 20 scenes)

➡ See Section ④ below for a full comparison of the 3 free tools. Beginners: start with Google Vids.

Camera Language

Slow push-in for emotional scenes · Wide shot for establishing · Close-up for reactions · Static hold for narration-heavy scenes.

Transitions

Fade in/out · Cross dissolve · Cut. Choose 1–2 styles only and keep them consistent throughout the video.

Story Flow Check

Calm village → sadness → kindness → magic → new city → romance → conflict → happy ending. The emotional arc must be clear without audio.

Recommended Tool

Start with Google Vids for assembly. Use CapCut for effects and transitions. Use Canva for title cards and scene labels.

Phase 04 — Audio Finalization

Take your silent draft video from Phase 03 and add narration, background music and sound effects. This phase makes your video classroom-ready. Do not rush — audio quality decides whether the video feels professional or unfinished.

  • Step 1: Write a short narration line for each of the 20 scenes (max 2 sentences each)
  • Step 2: Record your voice-over, or use CapCut's AI Voice feature (free)
  • Step 3: Import the draft video into CapCut and add the voice-over track
  • Step 4: Add background music from CapCut's free music library (match the scene mood)
  • Step 5: Add optional sound effects — river, magic chimes, café ambience
  • Step 6: Balance audio — voice-over must always be louder than background music
  • Step 7: Add subtitles using CapCut's Auto Caption feature, then export final MP4
✅ Output: Final Complete Video with Voice-over, Music & Subtitles

➡ See Section ⑥ for the full Audio Guide — sample voice-over scripts and music mood directions for all 20 scenes.

Voice-over

Short, clear narration per scene. Max 2 sentences. Record in a quiet room. CapCut's AI Voice is a free alternative to recording.

Music Moods

Village calm → magic sparkle → café romance → dramatic conflict → hopeful resolution. Change music at each major emotional shift.

Audio Balance Rule

Voice-over volume: 80–100%. Background music: 20–30%. Sound effects: 40–50%. This ratio keeps narration clear throughout.

Final Export Check

Export as MP4 · Add subtitles · Add title card · Test playback on a phone before submitting · Upload to Google Drive and share the link.

Your Phase 01 output. Copy this prompt, personalise it with your own story and characters, then paste it into Gemini or ChatGPT to generate a complete 20-scene production guide.

How to Use This Prompt

1. Read the full prompt in the box on the right. Understand each section before copying.

2. Personalise the story section. Replace the Bawang Putih & Bawang Merah story with your own characters, plot and moral value if your project differs.

3. Adjust the format if needed — change duration, number of scenes or language to match your assignment.

4. Click "Copy" on the prompt box, then open gemini.google.com (free) or chatgpt.com (free tier) and paste the prompt.

5. Save the AI output. The response will include — for every scene — an image prompt, video prompt, camera movement, voice-over line and music suggestion. This is your production bible for Phases 02, 03 and 04.

⚠ Important: Do not skip this step. Without a Master Prompt output, you will not have the image prompts needed for Phase 02 or the voice-over lines needed for Phase 04.

⑥ Phase 04: Audio Finalization Guide

The final production phase. Your silent draft from Phase 03 is brought to life with narration, music and sound. Use CapCut (free) as your main audio tool — it handles voice-over, music, captions and export in one place.

Audio Workflow — Step by Step

Follow these steps in order after your Phase 03 silent draft is ready. Do not add audio directly in Google Vids if your draft is already there — export it first, then bring it into CapCut for audio work.

  • Step 1 — Write narration: Write one short voice-over line per scene (max 2 sentences, under 9 seconds each). Use your Phase 01 Master Prompt output as the source.
  • Step 2 — Record or generate voice: Record your own voice in a quiet room, or use CapCut → AI Voice to generate a narrator voice automatically from your script.
  • Step 3 — Add background music: Use CapCut's free music library. Match music mood to each scene's emotion. Change the music track when the emotion shifts significantly.
  • Step 4 — Add sound effects: Optional but effective — river sounds, magic chimes, café noise or city ambience. Keep them subtle so they don't compete with narration.
  • Step 5 — Balance audio levels: Voice-over = loudest. Background music = low. Sound effects = medium. Listen through headphones to check the balance.
  • Step 6 — Add captions: Use CapCut → Auto Caption to generate subtitles automatically. Edit any errors before exporting.
  • Step 7 — Export final video: Export as MP4 (1080p). Test playback on a phone. Upload to Google Drive and copy the shareable link for submission.
✅ Output: Final Complete Video — Voice-over, Music, Captions & Export Done

Voice-over Rule

Max 2 sentences per 9-second scene. Record in a quiet space. Speak clearly and at a steady pace. Start speaking 0.5 seconds after the scene begins.

Music Mood Map

Scenes 1–5: calm village · Scenes 6–10: tense + magical · Scenes 11–16: hopeful + romantic · Scenes 17–18: dramatic · Scenes 19–20: uplifting & warm.

Audio Balance

Voice-over: 80–100% · Music: 20–30% · SFX: 40–50%. If the music drowns out the narrator, lower the music track — never lower the voice-over.

CapCut Features to Use

AI Voice · Auto Caption · Music Library · Sound Effects Library · Text-to-Speech · 1080p MP4 Export. All free.

Music Mood Guide by Scene Group

🌅 Scenes 1–5 — Village Opening: Soft Malay flute, light piano and gentle strings. Calm, warm, nostalgic. Keep volume very low — let the visuals breathe.

😢 Scenes 3–4 — Sadness & Hard Work: Slow, melancholic piano with soft strings. Slightly slower tempo. Conveys patience and hardship without being dramatic.

✨ Scenes 8–10 — Magic & Transformation: Chimes, celesta and rising strings. Build volume gradually as the magical box opens and the portal appears.

☕ Scenes 13–16 — Modern City & Romance: Warm piano, light acoustic guitar and soft strings. Cosy café feeling. Gentle and sweet without being dramatic.

⚡ Scenes 17–18 — Conflict: Dramatic strings, low cello and subtle percussion. Slightly faster tempo. Tension without being scary or overwhelming.

🌸 Scenes 19–20 — Hopeful Ending: Uplifting piano, full strings and a soft choir or pad layer. Fade out slowly over the moral value text card.
